Four Ways to Navigate

An Empirical Study of Vision-and-Language Navigation Policies on a Single GPU

Shubh Jain

September 2026 · technical report

Paper (PDF) Code Model cards Demos BibTeX

TL;DR

We built and compared four ways to turn a pretrained vision-language or video model into a policy that follows English route instructions in unseen buildings (R2R-CE, Habitat), each trained on one 16 GB GPU. Supervising where to go with image-space pointing gave the best true success, 31.3 %. A fine-tuned video reasoner reached the goal region most often, 47.5 %, but almost never stopped there. A from-scratch reimplementation of LatentPilot failed the method's own gate at this scale. Across all four, stopping, not reaching, is the bottleneck.

Abstract

We study four ways of turning a pretrained vision-language or video model into an instruction-following navigation policy for Vision-and-Language Navigation in continuous environments (R2R-CE). Every model is trained with low-rank adapters on one 16 GB consumer GPU:

  • a from-scratch reimplementation of LatentPilot, whose Pilot Token is trained to predict the visual embedding two steps ahead and is fed back as a recurrent latent;
  • a waypoint-pointing policy whose image-space prediction drives a bearing controller;
  • the pretrained action stream of the Cosmos3-Edge video world model, used zero-shot as a diffusion policy;
  • the reasoning tower of the same model, fine-tuned to output the next command from eight seconds of egocentric video.

At this scale the LatentPilot objective fails the method's own gate. The learned Pilot Token predicts future frames 2.25× better than an identity control, yet reaches the goal less often (10.7 % vs. 17.3 %). We trace this to a loss balance that drifts by an order of magnitude during training.

Pointing supervision benefits from label corrections, more data, frame history and reading STOP from intermediate layers. With these it reaches a strict self-stop success of 31.3 % (SPL 25.4) on 150 unseen episodes. The zero-shot diffusion policy recovers ego-motion from video but follows full instructions only weakly. The fine-tuned reasoner reaches the goal region most often (47.5 %) but almost never stops there.

Key findings

  1. A better predictor was a worse navigator. LatentPilot's auxiliary loss is balanced only at initialisation. By the end of training it takes 5.5× the action loss's weight.
  2. Offline probes picked the wrong model twice. The same happened with a zero-shot layout probe and with next-action accuracy for the reasoner. Rank policies closed-loop.
  3. STOP lives mid-network. A linear probe finds it at AUC 0.72 in layer 15, but 0.44 (below chance) at the output layer.
  4. The pointing policy under-turns. Its predicted bearing is 0.42× the true bearing, so turns are missed as FORWARD.
  5. Data first. Going from 6 to 16 buildings tripled strict success. Fixing three silent label bugs took label/expert agreement from 0.69 to 0.97.
